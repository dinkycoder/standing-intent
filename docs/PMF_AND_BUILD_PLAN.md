## TL;DR
- **Build a Level-3-autonomy "Standing-Intent digital-goods procurement agent" for API/data/compute purchases settled via x402 on Base — NOT a consumer shopping/checkout, ticketing, or reservation agent.** Consumer retail checkout is already being closed by card networks (Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay) and OpenAI/Stripe's ACP, and the incumbents' own numbers show consumers don't yet want in-chat autonomous buying (Walmart EVP Daniel Danker said in-chat purchases "converted at one-third the rate of click-out transactions"; OpenAI confirmed in March 2026 it was phasing out Instant Checkout). Ticketing is legally fatal (BOTS Act), reservations are increasingly illegal (NY Anti-Piracy Act), and general web checkout requires scraping that bot-defenses and the CFAA make brittle and risky.
- **The 10x is carried by ONE dimension: human touchpoints per completed transaction — a step-function from N confirmations to one upfront authorization and zero thereafter — enabled specifically by Base Spend Permissions, which card rails and 3-D Secure/SCA cannot replicate.** For machine-to-machine/API purchases, x402 also delivers genuine 10x on cost-per-transaction (sub-cent gas on Base vs. card interchange that makes sub-dollar payments impossible) and on latency-to-settlement.
- **PMF verdict: consumer demand for autonomous commerce is weak and unproven today; the real, present demand is machine-to-machine — agents paying for APIs, data, and compute.** That is where x402 already shows real volume on Base and where a small team can win. Build there.

## Key Findings

**The rails are real and production-ready — this is the strongest part of the thesis.**
- **x402** (Coinbase, launched May 2025; V2 launched December 2025) is an open HTTP-402 stablecoin payment protocol governed by the x402 Foundation (under the Linux Foundation, co-founded with Cloudflare, September 2025). The Coinbase facilitator supports Base, Solana, and other networks; settlement is predominantly USDC via EIP-3009 gasless transfers. Coinbase runs a free public facilitator (free tier ~1,000 tx/month). Spec is on GitHub at coinbase/x402.
- **Base Spend Permissions** (coinbase/spend-permissions, live) let a user sign an EIP-712 permission delegating a capped, recurring, revocable allowance (native + ERC-20 like USDC) to a spender address. Critically: the app cannot make arbitrary calls — scope is tightly limited to spending within the approved allowance, and users can revoke any time via SpendPermissionManager.revoke. This is the non-custodial mechanism enabling unattended agent spend. Sub Accounts add hierarchical ownership.
- **Commerce Payments Protocol** (base/commerce-payments, launched mid-2025 with Shopify) brings authorize-and-capture escrow onchain with six operations (authorize, capture, charge, void, reclaim, refund). It powers Shopify's USDC-on-Base payment option via a non-custodial escrow contract between payer and receiver.
- **Coinbase AgentKit / CDP** is real and maintained. CDP Server Wallets v2 (GA July 24, 2025) provide MPC-secured keys in AWS Nitro Enclaves. AgentKit is the framework; SmartWalletProvider works on Base (base-sepolia and base-mainnet). "Agentic Wallets" (branded product, reportedly Feb 11, 2026) add session caps, per-tx limits, gasless settlement on Base, and native x402.
- **ERC-8004** ("Trustless Agents": identity, reputation, validation registries) is a DRAFT ERC (no. 8004, August 2025; authors include Coinbase's Erik Reppel and Google's Jordan Ellis). Registry contracts exist on Base mainnet and Base Sepolia (canonical addresses beginning 0x8004…). It is early: core registries deployed on Ethereum mainnet January 29, 2026 (per Forbes, Feb 5, 2026, which noted 10,000+ agents and 20,000+ feedback entries during the prior ~3-month testnet), and Cambrian Network (via Nevermined) reports 24,000 agents registered since late January 2026 — but it is NOT battle-tested and does NOT itself solve "merchants block bots" for consumer retail.

**The RFB's "ERC-7496" reference is technically incoherent for checkout — flag it honestly.** ERC-7496 is "NFT Dynamic Traits," a DRAFT standard extending ERC-721/ERC-1155 with on-chain get/set trait methods, authored by OpenSea-affiliated engineers, designed to be enforced during Seaport order fulfillment (SIP-15) to prevent trait front-running. It has nothing intrinsic to do with "onchain checkout volumes" for everyday commerce. The RFB almost certainly conflated standards. The coherent standards for the RFB's intent are: x402 (payments), Commerce Payments Protocol (escrow/checkout), ERC-8004 (agent identity), and EIP-3009/Permit2 (token authorization). A commerce agent would only touch ERC-7496 if selling NFTs whose redemption state matters. Recommend acknowledging the ambiguity to the Fund rather than pretending to build to ERC-7496.

**Non-crypto rails have largely closed consumer retail checkout.**
- **OpenAI/Stripe Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP)** + Instant Checkout launched September 2025 (Etsy first, Shopify merchants to follow), open-sourced under Apache 2.0, with PayPal's ACP server bringing more SMBs. BUT: OpenAI confirmed in March 2026 it was phasing out Instant Checkout in favor of merchant-handled, app-based checkout (Search Engine Land, March 2026). Walmart made ~200,000 products available via OpenAI Instant Checkout starting November 2025; only ~30 Shopify merchants were live as of February 2026 (Forrester's Emily Pfeiffer). Walmart EVP of Product & Design Daniel Danker told Morgan Stanley's TMT conference (March 4, 2026) that in-chat purchases "converted at one-third the rate of click-out transactions" and called the experience "unsatisfying"; one analysis (BuildMVPFast) put ChatGPT Instant Checkout at a 1.18% conversion rate with 77.45% cart abandonment versus a 2.5–3.0% industry average.
- **Google AP2** (Agent Payments Protocol, September 2025) with 60+ partners; uses x402 as its stablecoin rail via the A2A x402 extension (co-built with Coinbase, Ethereum Foundation, MetaMask).
- **Visa Intelligent Commerce / Trusted Agent Protocol** and **Mastercard Agent Pay** both use tokenized card credentials with agent-identity binding and per-merchant/per-session limits. Mastercard Agent Pay launched April 29, 2025 with partners Microsoft, IBM, Salesforce, and Checkout.com; CEO Michael Miebach said on the Oct 30, 2025 earnings call, "Our first agentic transaction took place on our network this quarter," with Citi and US Bank first and all US cardholders enabled by mid-November 2025. Visa's Intelligent Commerce Connect supports four agent protocols including x402.
- Consensus (Forbes, June 2026): of the five most prominent agentic-commerce deployments live by early 2026, three settle on card rails and two on stablecoins — split cleanly along the consumer-retail-vs-machine-payment line. **Cards win consumer retail; stablecoins win machine-to-machine.**

**Autonomy baseline (measured): today's agents are unreliable on multi-step transactions.**
- τ-bench (Sierra, 2024): frozen official board tops at Claude 3.5 Sonnet, pass^1 retail 69.2%, airline 46.0%; GPT-4o retail 60.4%, airline 42.0%. The pass^k reliability metric collapses: GPT-4o retail falls from ~60% (pass^1) toward ~25% (pass^8). Production users don't retry — so real reliability is the pass^k number.
- WebArena: rose from ~14% to 61.7% (IBM CUGA, Feb 2025); human performance is 78%. WebChoreArena (harder): top models ~37.8%.
- WebVoyager (live sites, filtered subsets): SOTA scaffolds 85–92% (Surfer-H 92.2% at $0.13/task); but OpenAI Operator 61%, and on Amazon specifically 0% in one AgentOccam/Agent-E table.
- OSWorld (real computer tasks): OpenAI Operator reported ~38% success (62% failure) in 2026; Anthropic Computer Use ~72%.
- Real-world checkout: Operator was slow, failed on CAPTCHAs/auth/account-creation; conversion "dismal." A reported 73% reading-failure rate from dynamic UI elements and 36% CAPTCHA-handling failure with Claude Sonnet 4.5 in one study.
- Human baseline: Baymard Institute ("50 Cart Abandonment Rate Statistics," updated Sept 22, 2025) — "we've calculated the average cart abandonment rate of 70.22%... based on 50 different studies"; the same page notes the average US checkout flow "contains 23.48 form elements... (14.88 if only counting the form fields)"; unexpected costs are the #1 abandonment cause (48%).

**Competitive field.** Skyfire ($9.5M total from Neuberger Berman, a16z CSX, Coinbase Ventures; founded 2023; KYA "Know Your Agent" + Agent Checkout, thousands of tx/day). Crossmint (agentic checkout, merchant-of-record, guardrail wallets, 150+ countries). Basis Theory ($33M Series B, leads Agentic Commerce Consortium). Nekuda ($5M seed, Madrona/Amex/Visa Ventures). Rye ($14M, universal checkout). Nevermined, Catena Labs, PayOS, Payman, Halliday also active. The payments/identity layer is crowded and well-funded; a solo builder should NOT try to be a payments-rail or identity company.

**Legal screen.**
- **Event ticketing — FATAL.** BOTS Act of 2016 prohibits circumventing access controls/purchase limits on ticket sites; first FTC/DOJ enforcement in 2021 ($3.7M paid of a $31M+ judgment against three NY brokers). An agent buying tickets by evading limits or CAPTCHAs directly violates it. Avoid.
- **Reservations — increasingly illegal / closed.** NY Restaurant Reservation Anti-Piracy Act (signed Dec 19, 2024) bans third-party reservation platforms without written restaurant agreement; killed Appointment Trader's NY model. OpenTable/Resy deploy anti-bot defenses (credit-card holds, 24/7 fraud teams, AI detection). No open agent API. Avoid.
- **General web shopping — brittle & risky.** Scraping merchant sites implicates the CFAA (Van Buren narrowed it; hiQ v. LinkedIn allows scraping public data, but bypassing bot defenses/auth is different) plus Cloudflare/Akamai/DataDome bot-blocking. ACP/AP2 official channels remove the need to scrape — but those are card-rail channels, not a Base opportunity.
- **Coupons — value-extractive, litigated.** The Honey/PayPal affiliate-diversion saga (In re PayPal Honey Browser Extension Litigation; motion to dismiss DENIED June 22, 2026 on ECPA/CFAA/CIPA/UCL claims) shows coupon/affiliate mechanics invite litigation. Not a durable base for a venture.
- **Money transmission — the good news.** A strictly non-custodial architecture avoids FinCEN MSB status. FinCEN's 2019 CVC Guidance is explicit: unhosted wallet providers, multisig providers without unilateral control, and DApp developers are NOT money transmitters; and "insofar as the person conducting a transaction through the unhosted wallet is doing so to purchase goods or services on the user's own behalf, they are not a money transmitter" (31 CFR 1010.100(ff)). WHAT BREAKS IT: holding/pooling user funds, taking custody, having unilateral control of keys, or acting as an intermediary that takes a cut inside the flow. Design rule: user funds live in the user's own Base Account; the agent spends via a capped Spend Permission; the venture never touches funds and monetizes via subscription or a fee billed OUTSIDE the payment flow.

## Details

### The four constraints and how the recommendation satisfies them
1. **Gradeable AI artifact at the core** — yes: the artifact is an autonomous planning-execution agent with a policy-enforcement layer and a trained/tuned component (see Deliverable D/E), evaluated against a named public baseline (τ-bench-style tool-agent eval adapted to a digital-goods procurement environment).
2. **13-week MVP by a small team** — yes: digital-goods/API procurement needs NO shipping, returns, physical inventory, or scraping; the "merchant" is an x402 endpoint, so the two-sided market collapses to software-on-both-sides.
3. **Credible to Base Ecosystem Fund** — yes: uses x402, Base Spend Permissions, CDP AgentKit, optionally ERC-8004 and Commerce Payments Protocol — all Base-native primitives the Fund is explicitly funding.
4. **Scalable to a solo/small-team vertical venture** — yes: an agent that manages a budget across many API/data/compute vendors is a horizontal capability with vertical wedge markets (research agents buying data, trading agents buying signals, media agents buying generation credits).

### Deliverable A — PMF Verdict
**Consumer demand for fully autonomous purchasing is weak and unproven; supply-side infrastructure is racing ahead of a use case consumers have not yet embraced.** The clearest evidence is that the best-resourced player on earth failed: OpenAI launched Operator (Jan 2025), deprecated it, launched Instant Checkout (Sept 2025), and confirmed in March 2026 it was phasing it out — after Walmart's Daniel Danker reported ~3x worse in-chat conversion and only ~30 Shopify merchants went live. Salesforce's own survey (48% of AI-shoppers "open to" an agent buying) measures willingness, not behavior. **Where demand is real today: machine-to-machine — agents paying for APIs, data, and compute via x402.** That is a genuine, present, growing market (x402 processing real volume on Base; Google AP2 using x402 as its M2M rail; Anthropic MCP, NEAR AI, OpenMind, Browserbase, Freepik, Yield.xyz as real x402 consumers/providers). It is the one segment where the buyer is software that does not abandon carts, does not need chargebacks, and pays sub-dollar amounts cards cannot serve. Encouragingly, Chainalysis ("Inside x402: 100M Agentic Payments on Base," June 3, 2026) reports $1+ transactions grew to 95% of x402 volume (up from 49% in early 2025), a signal of maturing real usage rather than pure gaming.

### Deliverable B — Vertical Recommendation
**PRIMARY: Agentic purchasing of digital/API goods (machine-to-machine commerce) settled via x402 on Base — framed within the RFB's "agentic shopping and checkout" segment but for the digital-goods/API sub-market.** This is the least-crowded winnable space for a small team because the incumbents (Skyfire, Crossmint, Basis Theory) are building the payments/identity RAILS, not the intent-to-outcome AGENT that decides what to buy, enforces a budget, and proves it got the best price. The venture sits one layer up: the autonomous buyer.

**FALLBACK: A "coupon/discount" reframe as an economic-optimization agent for digital goods** — an agent that, given a standing budget and intent, comparison-shops across x402/stablecoin-accepting vendors and provably captures the best available price. This stays inside the RFB's "coupon and discount agents" bucket without the Honey-style affiliate-diversion legal exposure (it never rewrites affiliate links; it optimizes the user's own spend).

**ELIMINATED:**
- **Event ticketing** — BOTS Act makes the core action (circumventing purchase limits/access controls) a federal violation. Non-starter.
- **Booking & reservations** — NY Anti-Piracy Act + platform anti-bot posture + no open agent API. Closing fast, state by state.
- **General consumer retail checkout** — closed by card networks + ACP/AP2; requires scraping that is brittle and CFAA-risky; incumbents proved consumers don't want it yet.

### Deliverable C — The 10x Autonomy Specification

Proposed **Levels of Commerce-Agent Autonomy (LoCA)**, analogous to SAE:
- **L0 Suggestion-only** — recommends, human does everything (most 2024 chatbots).
- **L1 Assisted fill** — pre-fills carts/forms, human reviews & pays.
- **L2 Supervised execution** — executes with per-step confirmation (Operator, ChatGPT agent, most card-rail agent pilots sit here — every payment still needs a human tap/passkey).
- **L3 Conditional autonomy** — ONE upfront authorization (a scoped budget + policy), then exception-only escalation. **← BUILD TARGET.**
- **L4 Unattended standing-intent** — recurring/triggered execution under policy with no per-purchase human contact.
- **L5 Fully delegated budget management** — agent manages an entire budget across goals autonomously.

Incumbents sit at **L2** because the payment authorization is the hard wall: card rails require SCA/3-D Secure/passkey per transaction (Mastercard Agent Pay and Visa TAP bind tokens but still lean on consumer approval flows). **Base Spend Permissions move the authorization to ONE upfront signature, structurally enabling L3–L4.** That is the mechanism, and it is not replicable on card rails.

**The 10x threshold table** (incumbent → target; the single carrying dimension in bold):

| Dimension | Incumbent baseline (measured/sourced) | 10x target | Realistic? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Human touchpoints per completed transaction** | **~1 approval per purchase (card-rail agents, L2); N confirmations in web-agent flows** | **1 upfront authorization, then 0 for the budget's life (dozens–hundreds of purchases)** | **YES — carries the 10x. Spend Permissions make this structural, not incremental.** |
| Cost per completed transaction (M2M) | Card interchange floor (~$0.05–0.30 + %); sub-dollar API payments uneconomic | Sub-cent gas on Base + free CDP facilitator tier | YES for micropayments — card rails literally cannot serve this |
| Latency intent→settled | Card auth+capture; multi-day merchant settlement | Seconds to onchain finality on Base | YES for settlement finality |
| End-to-end completion w/o intervention | τ-bench pass^1 retail ~69%, pass^8 ~25%; Operator OSWorld ~38% | 90%+ pass^1 in a constrained digital-goods domain | PARTIAL — achievable only by narrowing the domain to structured API purchases, not open web |
| Success under adversarial conditions (CAPTCHA/login/price change) | 36% CAPTCHA-handling failure; 73% dynamic-UI reading failure (Sonnet 4.5 study) | No CAPTCHAs/logins exist in x402/API flows | YES — but only because the domain removes the adversary, not because the agent is smarter |
| Economic outcome (best price captured) | No incumbent proves best-price capture | Provable best-price across ≥N vendors, logged onchain | Differentiator, not 10x |
| Constraint-adherence / policy-violation rate | Not systematically measured | 0 budget breaches (enforced on-chain by Spend Permission cap) | YES — hard guarantee, not best-effort |

**Honest conclusion:** a true 10x on the raw AI-reliability dimension (task completion) is NOT plausible against open-web incumbents — that is a model-capability race a solo student cannot win. **The 10x is carried by human-touchpoints-per-transaction, made real by Base Spend Permissions, and reinforced by cost/latency on M2M micropayments.** The proving experiment: run the agent against a fixed basket of digital-goods procurement tasks; measure human touchpoints to complete the whole basket under (a) a card-rail/L2 agent and (b) the Spend-Permission L3 agent; show the ratio is ≥10:1 while budget-adherence stays perfect.

### Deliverable D — Product design for genuine autonomy

Architecture ("Standing-Intent Procurement Agent"), NOT a chatbot-fills-a-cart:
- **Intent capture / specification model.** User expresses a *durable, constraint-bearing intent* as a structured "mandate": goal, budget cap, time window, allowed vendor/category allowlist, per-transaction max, quality thresholds. This is compiled into (a) an off-chain policy object and (b) an on-chain Spend Permission (EIP-712 signature) that hard-caps total spend. This is the key differentiator: intent is durable and policy-bearing, not a single-turn prompt.
- **Planning/execution loop.** Planner decomposes the mandate into candidate purchases; queries the x402 Bazaar / vendor registry for options; evaluates price/quality; executes via x402 (HTTP 402 → EIP-3009 signature → CDP facilitator settles on Base). Planner-executor-memory pattern (the architecture the WebArena literature credits for the 14%→62% jump).
- **Tool/action surface.** x402 client; CDP AgentKit wallet actions; Bazaar discovery; a price/quality comparator; (optional) ERC-8004 reputation lookups to filter vendors.
- **Policy-and-constraint enforcement (two layers).** On-chain: Spend Permission cap = cannot exceed budget even if the agent is buggy or hijacked (hard guarantee). Off-chain guardrails: allowlist checks, anomaly/price-sanity checks, idempotency keys to prevent double-buys, per-vendor rate limits.
- **Failure detection, retry, recovery.** Detect: HTTP 402 mismatches, settlement failures, price-above-threshold, out-of-policy vendor. Recover: retry with backoff, try next-best vendor, degrade gracefully. Escalate ONLY when: budget exhausted, no in-policy vendor found, or repeated settlement failure.
- **Escalation policy (minimize the count).** Default is silent execution within policy; escalate by exception only. The metric optimized is "human touchpoints per completed basket."
- **Memory & personalization.** Vector store of past purchases, vendor reliability, price history → informs future best-price decisions.
- **Evaluation harness.** A reproducible digital-goods procurement environment (synthetic + real x402 endpoints) scored on completion rate, human-touchpoints, budget-adherence, best-price capture, cost/tx; pass^1 and pass^k reported (following the τ-bench reliability discipline).

### Deliverable E — 13-Week Build Plan (student's actual stack)

**AI artifact (graded as AI Engineering):** a multi-component agent, not a prompt wrapper. Core = LangChain agent (planner-executor) on an LLM (Claude via API). **Trained component to satisfy AI-project rigor:** a fine-tuned/trained *vendor-selection & price-anomaly model* — e.g., a scikit-learn/PyTorch classifier (logistic regression / random forest baseline, optionally a small fine-tuned HF model) that predicts, from vendor features + price history, whether an offer is "accept / reject / escalate." This gives a measurable ML artifact with train/test metrics, distinct from the LLM. Evaluation harness must beat a named baseline: a **ReAct/GPT-4o-style single-agent tool-caller** on the same procurement tasks (τ-bench is the methodological template; the numbers to beat are pass^1/pass^k on the custom env).

- **Wk 1 — Kill/confirm the riskiest assumption first.** Highest-risk assumption: *enough real x402-payable endpoints exist to compose a non-trivial multi-vendor procurement basket on Base testnet/mainnet.* Cheap experiment: in week 1, hit the x402 Bazaar + the reference weather API + 2–3 known endpoints (Prixe stock prices, an image-gen endpoint) from a script; confirm discovery → 402 → USDC settlement on Base Sepolia end-to-end. If <~5 usable endpoints exist, pivot the basket to a synthetic sandbox of self-hosted x402 endpoints (Flask + x402 middleware) — still a valid artifact, lower external risk. **Verify testnet reality:** x402 facilitator + Spend Permissions + CDP Smart Wallets all support base-sepolia; ERC-8004 registries are on Base Sepolia. (This was a prior gotcha — confirmed testnet-available.)
- **Wk 2 — Env + eval harness.** Build the procurement eval environment (JSON task specs, database-state grading à la τ-bench), CI skeleton (GitHub Actions), trace logging (Langfuse, open-source, self-host, OTel). Define baseline agent.
- **Wk 3 — Wallet + payments spine.** CDP Server Wallet v2 / SmartWalletProvider on base-sepolia; x402 client; execute a real testnet USDC payment. Flask API scaffolding; PostgreSQL schema (mandates, purchases, vendor history); Docker.
- **Wk 4 — Spend Permission integration.** Implement EIP-712 Spend Permission sign + register + spend + revoke flow; prove on-chain budget cap is enforced. This is the crux demo.
- **Wk 5 — Planner-executor agent v1.** LangChain planner decomposes mandate; executor discovers + buys; memory in vector DB.
- **Wk 6 — Policy/guardrail layer.** Allowlists, idempotency, price-sanity, escalation logic. Off-chain + on-chain enforcement wired.
- **Wk 7 — Trained model v1.** Collect/generate vendor+price data; train scikit-learn accept/reject/escalate classifier; integrate; report train/test metrics.
- **Wk 8 — Failure/recovery + retries.** Adversarial cases (settlement fail, price spike, vendor down); measure recovery-without-escalation rate.
- **Wk 9 — Eval run #1 vs baseline.** Full harness: completion, human-touchpoints, budget-adherence, best-price, cost/tx, pass^1/pass^k. Iterate.
- **Wk 10 — UI + observability.** Streamlit (or minimal web) mandate-builder + run-trace viewer; evals-in-CI gating; performance monitoring.
- **Wk 11 — Hardening + AWS deploy.** Dockerized services to AWS (serverless/ECS per coursework); secrets; idempotency under load.
- **Wk 12 — Eval run #2 + 10x experiment.** The touchpoints-ratio experiment (L2 card-style vs L3 Spend-Permission) producing the headline 10x number.
- **Wk 13 — Public GitHub demo + writeup.** Repo, README, reproducible evals, recorded end-to-end run on Base.

### Deliverable G — The 10x statement (for a Base Ecosystem Fund partner)
"Today, the best consumer purchasing agents — OpenAI's ChatGPT agent, and card-network agents on Visa Intelligent Commerce and Mastercard Agent Pay — sit at Level 2 autonomy: every single payment still requires a human approval, because card rails cannot delegate spend without SCA/3-D Secure per transaction. Our agent uses Base Spend Permissions to collapse that to ONE upfront authorization and zero touchpoints thereafter across an entire budget of dozens to hundreds of machine-to-machine purchases — a ≥10x reduction in human touchpoints per completed transaction, with a hard on-chain budget cap that guarantees zero policy violations. We will substantiate it with a head-to-head experiment: the same digital-goods procurement basket run under a card-rail-style L2 agent versus our Spend-Permission L3 agent, measuring human touchpoints to completion and budget-adherence — a ratio we expect to exceed 10:1 while adherence stays perfect."

## Recommendations
1. **Build the Standing-Intent digital-goods/API procurement agent on Base with x402 + Spend Permissions.** Target Level-3 autonomy. This is the single winnable, legal, 13-week-feasible, Base-native, AI-gradeable option.
2. **Week-1 gate:** confirm ≥5 real x402 endpoints compose a meaningful basket; if not, fall back to a self-hosted synthetic x402 sandbox (Flask + x402 middleware). Either way the artifact stands.
3. **Do NOT build:** ticketing (BOTS Act), reservations (NY Anti-Piracy + closing), open-web consumer checkout (card networks + ACP/AP2 own it; scraping is CFAA-risky), or a payments/identity rail (Skyfire/Crossmint/Basis Theory are funded and ahead).
4. **Non-custodial by construction:** user funds stay in the user's Base Account; agent spends via capped Spend Permission; venture never holds/pools funds and monetizes via subscription or a fee billed outside the payment flow — this keeps you out of FinCEN MSB status and FL money-transmitter licensing.
5. **Frame ERC-7496 honestly to the Fund:** name that it is NFT Dynamic Traits and not a checkout standard, and state you are building to the coherent Base commerce stack (x402, Commerce Payments Protocol, ERC-8004, EIP-3009).
6. **Benchmarks that would change the plan:** if a card network ships true one-authorization-many-purchases delegation for consumers (watch Visa TAP / Mastercard Agentic Tokens spend-limit scopes), the touchpoints 10x narrows — pivot harder into micropayment/M2M cost advantage. If consumer autonomous-buy adoption suddenly proves out (watch ChatGPT app-based checkout revival metrics), revisit consumer retail. If ERC-8004 reputation gets real adoption (watch registered-agent growth beyond the reported ~24k), add vendor-reputation gating as a moat.

### Business model & unit economics
Comparable companies monetize via take rate (Crossmint/merchant-of-record), per-call fee (Nevermined metering), subscription, or SaaS (Basis Theory vaulting). **For this venture, the non-custodial constraint forces a fee OUTSIDE the payment flow: a SaaS subscription per active mandate, or a fee billed separately (never a cut skimmed inside the USDC transfer).** Illustrative unit economics per completed procurement transaction: LLM inference + tool calls (a few cents to low-tens-of-cents depending on planning depth) + Base gas (fractions of a cent) + USDC transfer (gasless via EIP-3009, facilitator free tier). Gross margin is high because the dominant cost is LLM tokens, not payments infrastructure; viability depends on volume of managed spend × subscription price, not on transaction take. This is why M2M/API procurement — high-frequency, low-value, software-on-both-sides — is the right shape: it maximizes the number of transactions a single mandate generates.

## Caveats
- **Fast-moving landscape (dates matter).** Key facts are 2025–early 2026: x402 V2 (Dec 2025), ACP wind-down (announced March 2026), Mastercard Agent Pay full US rollout (Nov 2025). "Agentic Wallets" Feb 2026 launch date comes from a secondary source and was NOT confirmable against a Coinbase primary source within this research — treat as unverified.
- **x402 on-chain volume figures conflict and include gamed activity.** Chainalysis ("Inside x402," June 3, 2026) reports cumulative x402 on Base "well over 100 million by Q1 2026"; the 119M-tx/$35M-value Base figure traces to MEXC/Sherlock (March 2026); another source cites ~$50M cumulative. Critically, Artemis (Dec 2025) flagged ~48% of transaction counts and 81% of volume as gamed/wash-type activity (e.g., the PING pay-to-mint loop). Verify against Coinbase/Chainalysis primary data and discount gamed volume before quoting a single number.
- **ERC-8004 and ERC-7496 are DRAFT** ERCs, not final; ERC-8004 is early. The arXiv study "From Agent Identity to Agent Economy: Measuring the Operational Readiness of ERC-8004 AI Agents" (arXiv 2606.12128), analyzing agent IDs 0–9,999 over Jan 29–Apr 9, 2026, concludes early adoption is "registration-heavy but operationally shallow" — i.e., agents register but rarely transact. Do not overstate ERC-8004 as a solved trust layer.
- **The AI-reliability 10x is NOT claimed** — deliberately. The 10x rests on touchpoints/cost/latency structural advantages, not on beating frontier models at task completion.
- **The two-sided cold-start is real** even for M2M: it is mitigated (software on both sides, no shipping/returns) but not eliminated — the week-1 endpoint-availability check is make-or-break.
- **"Two x402 Bazaars"** exist: Coinbase's official CDP discovery layer (launched Sept 10, 2025; Coinbase self-describes it as "currently more like 'Yahoo search'"), and a separate community `x402bazaar.org` / `x402-bazaar` npm project (the source of the "112+ services" claim). Do not conflate them.
- **Legal analysis is not legal advice**; a Florida-based venture should obtain a formal money-transmission opinion before mainnet launch, especially if any fee is ever taken inside the payment flow. FTC Section 5 (unfair/deceptive practices), consumer-liability-for-agent-errors, and state auto-renewal/negative-option rules should also be reviewed once real consumer mandates are involved.
